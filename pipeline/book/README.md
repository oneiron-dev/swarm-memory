# pipeline/book: the data the book UI reads

The app reads as a book: one chapter per village goal, one page per day, a cast drawer per day. `book.py` writes the texts with a model (the real run used `gpt-5.6-luna`; any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, our prime-agent, or `--mock` for templated placeholders that pass every rule below); `pipeline/bundle/build_bundle.py` turns them into the served files `data/book.json` and `data/adherence/<agent>.json`, and computes `data/talk/<agent>.json` itself (docs/schema.md section 4). So every served string goes through the same redaction and validator as the rest of the bundle.

## What it does

- **Input.** The served bundle `bundle/data/` (already redacted): `bundle.json` days, goals and agents, `summaries/agent/`, `summaries/room/`, `summaries/village/<day>.json`, `sources/<day>.json`.
- **Chapters.** `build_bundle.book_chapters`: a day joins the chapter whose goal equals its `village_goal` exactly; a day without one joins the chapter of the day before it. `book.py` and the bundler use the same function.
- **`days`.** One paragraph per day. The material is the day's counts, its per-agent summaries, its room summaries and the first 6,000 characters of the AI Digest daily text. One cite table per day numbers every ref of those summaries that exists in `sources/<day>.json`, plus the AI Digest text's own ref; each summary's `[n]` is rewritten into that table. After the reply, a marker not in the table is dropped and counted (`dropped`); the kept cites are renumbered 1..k in order of use. A reply is accepted only with 80 to 130 words (counted without markers) and at least one valid marker; otherwise it is asked again (3 tries by default) with a line saying what was wrong, and a day with no accepted reply stays missing. A cached paragraph that fails this rule is asked again; it stays as it is unless a new reply passes. A day with no material gets a fixed line and no call (`"empty": true`).
- **`gaps`.** A day with the fixed line (no agent, room or AI Digest summary) gets one paragraph of 40 to 90 words built one level down, from its raw slice rows (`chat`, `memory`, `session_goal`, `session_summary`, `next_goal`; at most 150 lines, numbered as the summarizer does, each clipped at 800 characters; over 150 rows, every goal, summary and memory row stays and chat is sampled evenly), with the day prompt's rules and the same acceptance and retries. Its cites are slice rows; the bundler serves every cited row in `sources/<day>.json`. A day with no text rows gets a fixed placement line, no call: "The village was paused this day; the next entry is day N." (N = the next day with its own text). Records carry `"gap": true` or `"placement": true`.
- **`chapters`.** One title and paragraph per chapter from its day paragraphs, with the day cites renumbered into one chapter table (ref and day). Accepted only with a title of 3 to 6 words, 120 to 180 words and a valid marker; the same retries. Prompt versions (`--chapter-prompt`): `story-v2` (default since round 6: what the week was about and what stood in the way, the evidenced turn, what stayed unresolved; nothing manufactured; uncertainty kept) `story-v2-170` (story-v2 plus "Keep the prose under 170 words.", round 7, for chapters whose story-v2 reply ran long; pick them with `--only 1,3,...`) and `v1` (the first prompt; records without a `prompt` key). A chapter counts as done when it has an accepted reply under the chosen version; the replies of other versions stay in `chapters.jsonl` for comparison, and since the bundler serves the last accepted line per chapter, a chapter whose new reply fails (run it with `--content-attempts 2`) keeps its earlier text. Runs only for chapters whose days all have a paragraph.
- **`hooks`.** Per chapter, from the paragraph the bundler serves (the last line in `chapters.jsonl`, after its accepted rewordings) and the day of each of its cite numbers: a `hook` (6 to 14 words, a specific true thing from the paragraph, no question, no number the paragraph does not state), 3 to 6 `beats` (`{"text", "cites"}`, at most 24 words each, ending with the paragraph's own cite numbers) and a `figure` (`{"value", "label", "cites"}`: a number the paragraph states in digits, value at most 8 characters, label at most 7 words; or null when the paragraph has none). One prompt asks for one JSON object; each field is checked with the bundler's own rules (`hook_problem`, `beats_problem`, `figure_problem` in build_bundle.py); the fields that fail are asked once more with the reasons, and a field that fails twice is saved as null with its reason in `why` (the page then falls back). Beat markers are normalised as in summaries and a beat's `cites` are taken from its text. Stricter than the bundler, `book.py` also wants at most 3 cite numbers per beat and a figure whose number the paragraph states outside agent names and dates (no version numbers, days of the month or years), and it turns curly quotes straight and a dash between clauses into a comma (the page's copy rules). Prompt versions: `hooks-v5` (current: v4 plus the stranger rule, no unexplained acronym or internal name, no bare number first, no pronoun before its noun), `hooks-v4` (the hook built from one sentence of the paragraph, keeping its cause and effect), `hooks-v3`, `hooks-v2` and the `hooks-v1` pilot; their records stay in the file and the last line per chapter text wins. A line with a `picked` note was chosen by hand from several replies (or a figure edited by hand to keep the paragraph's qualifier) and appended last, so it is the one served. One line per chapter in `hooks.jsonl`, keyed by the hash of the chapter text it came from (`src`), so a rewritten chapter is asked again and an old record never applies to a new text. `--only 1,3` asks named chapters. The bundler applies the accepted rewordings (`hook`, `beat:<i>`, `figure_label`), redacts, and serves a field only when it still passes the rules beside the served text (else null, counted as `*_dropped`).
- **`pairs`.** Per day, the talking pairs the book page draws: `day_pairs` in build_bundle.py folds the served `views.interactions.edges` of both directions into unordered pairs, sums the day's weight and keeps the three strongest, in the page's order (web/src/book/scene.ts `pairsOf`; `human` is a member like any agent). For each pair, the chat rows between the two that day, as the bundler counts the edge: a row by one that names the other (the bundler's agent-name matcher) and both rows of a reply (the next message in the same room within 10 minutes from the other); oneiroNER span mentions and mentions in session notes are not read. Over 60 rows, the 60 nearest the middle of the clock hour that holds most of them. Luna gets the numbered lines (`[n] HH:MM speaker (#room): text`, humans as "a human") and writes one sentence of 8 to 16 words on what the two talked about, naming both (humans as "the humans"), with 1 to 3 line numbers; `book.py` maps the numbers to row refs (`chat:<id>`), turns curly quotes straight and a dash into a comma, and checks the sentence with the bundler's `pair_problem` and for both names. One retry with the reason; then the record is saved with `text` null and its `why` (the page falls back to "A talked with B"); a pair with no chat rows is saved null with no call. One line per pair in `pairs.jsonl`, keyed by day, pair and prompt version (`pairs-v1`); per pair the last line with a text is served. `--only 100,200` asks named days.
- **`stage`.** Per chapter with beats, from its paragraph and beats (as the bundler serves them, markers taken out; beats numbered from 0) and its active agents (ids and names): a stage direction for the village pen, `{"props": [{"kind", "label"}], "moments": [{"beat", "kind", "agents", "label"}]}`, with 1 to 3 props (`stall`, `mailbox`, `ballot`, `scaffold`, `gallery`, `chessboard`, `stage`, `press`, `garden`, `lanterns`, `cloud`, `signpost`: what the week was about) and 0 to 6 moments (`celebrate`, `build`, `send`, `vote`, `win`, `fail`, `gather`, `play`: only what the paragraph states happened, on the beat that tells it, with at most 4 of the chapter's agent ids, never the humans). A label is at most 3 plain words or null: no quotes or brackets, at most 32 characters, a number only when the paragraph states it. One prompt asks for one JSON object; it is checked with the bundler's `stage_problem` and, stricter, each prop kind at most once, at most one moment per beat and no label naming an agent; a failing reply is asked once more with the reason, and a stage that fails twice is saved as null with its reason in `why` (the page draws the board alone). Kinds are lowercased, an agent written by its name gets its id. One line per chapter in `stage.jsonl`, keyed by the hash of the chapter text (`src`) and of its cached beats (`beats_src`, `build_bundle.stage_key`), so new beats are asked again. Prompt versions: `stage-v2` (current: the big moments, usually 2 to 4, at most one per beat) and the `stage-v1` pilot (3 chapters), whose records stay in the file; the last line per key wins. `--only 1,3` asks named chapters.
- **`adherence`.** Every agent-day summary (text without `[n]`), 25 per call: `on`, `partly`, `off` or `idle` and a note of at most 15 words. The whole reply must parse as a JSON array (an object around it or prose beside it is a bad reply) of exactly the items sent, with integer indices (not booleans) and known scores; otherwise it is retried once; then its items go to `adherence-null.jsonl` (served as `"score": null`) and are tried again on the next run. A bad batch is never saved as done.
- **`rephrase`.** Runs the bundler's redaction for book text (the same `meta/` lists, the untagged rules) over every day text, chapter title, chapter text, hook, beat, figure label, pair sentence and adherence note. Where it would add a chip (`[human]`, `[email]`, `[id]`) that Luna's own text does not carry, Luna gets the sentences (or the title or note) that gain one (one call per day or chapter, 25 notes per call), with the words the filter cuts, and rewords them once with a plain synonym, keeping every fact and every `[n]`. The rewording is accepted only if the redaction then adds no chip and the cite numbers are the same; otherwise the redacted version stays. Each decision is one line in `rewrites.jsonl`, keyed by the hash of the exact text it was made from, so a rerun asks nothing twice and a rewrite never applies to a changed text. The bundler applies the accepted ones (in a chain) before its own redaction, which still runs. Run it after `days` and `chapters`, and again whenever the redaction lists change.
- **Calls.** Through `pipeline/common/swarm_llm.py` (summarize.py's caller): `--endpoint <url> --model <id>` (or `SWARM_LLM_BASE_URL`, `SWARM_LLM_MODEL`, `SWARM_LLM_API_KEY`), `--prime-agent <path>`, or `--mock`. The prompt is the material, the task line follows it; `hooks`, `pairs` and `stage` ask an endpoint for JSON mode. The run stops (exit 3) if a reply names another model than the one asked for. Transport errors back off (10 s doubling, 5 tries). Records carry the model that wrote them.
- **Cache.** Append-only JSONL in `book/`: `days.jsonl`, `chapters.jsonl`, `hooks.jsonl`, `pairs.jsonl`, `stage.jsonl`, `adherence.jsonl`, `adherence-null.jsonl`, `rewrites.jsonl`. A rerun does only what is missing; the last line per key wins. Before an append, a torn last line from a killed run is cut (or ended, when it is whole JSON). Dropped markers are counted with the same parser that drops them (`[2-5]` is four). Each line carries calls, tokens and seconds.

## Run

```bash
export SWARM_WORK=$W                     # the work area: reads $W/bundle/data, writes $W/book
export SWARM_LLM_BASE_URL=https://api.openai.com/v1 SWARM_LLM_MODEL=<model id> SWARM_LLM_API_KEY=<key>   # or --mock
for s in days gaps chapters hooks pairs stage; do python3 book.py $s --parallel 8; done
python3 book.py adherence --parallel 4
python3 book.py rephrase                 # after the lists change too
python3 book.py status                   # counts of the cache
python3 ../bundle/build_bundle.py        # emits book.json, pairs.json, adherence/, talk/
```

Flags: `--root` (default `$SWARM_WORK`), `--data` (default `<root>/bundle/data`), `--book` (default `<root>/book`), `--lists` (the redaction lists `rephrase` and `pairs` read; default `$SWARM_LISTS` or `<root>/meta`), `--parallel 8`, `--limit N` (new units), `--max-attempts 5`, `--content-attempts 3`, `--only`, `--chapter-prompt`, and the client flags `--mock`, `--endpoint`, `--model`, `--reasoning-effort`, `--prime-agent`, `--provider`, `--thinking`, `--timeout`. Python 3.12, stdlib only; it imports `pipeline/summarize/summarize.py`, `pipeline/bundle/build_bundle.py` and `pipeline/common/`. The real run went through prime-agent on Arch under `systemd-run`, one unit per step.

## Tests

`python3 -m unittest discover -s pipeline/book/tests` runs every step on a tiny synthetic bundle with a fake prime-agent (a short batch retried, a broken batch never saved, an invented marker dropped, a model mismatch stopping the run, a rewording accepted only when it passes the redaction, a stage retried once and then null, one pair sentence per talking pair) and the whole book once with `--mock`.

Real run data: AI Digest / AI Village. Research use only; nothing from the dataset is committed here.
